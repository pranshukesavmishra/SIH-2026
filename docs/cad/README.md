# ZeroDrift Mk3 — CAD

Parametric models of the build, written in OpenSCAD (free,
[openscad.org](https://openscad.org)) so they are text, diffable, and
regenerate from one source rather than being a binary someone has to
trust.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `geometry.scad` | The numbers the mechanism imposes, shared by the still model and the animation with `include` |
| `parts_lib.scad` | One module per BOM item, drawn at its real catalogue size |
| `zerodrift_full_assembly.scad` | Places those parts: rig, bench electronics, beacon, decoy |
| `animate.scad` | `$t` timeline: parts fly together, then the mechanism runs |
| `zerodrift_gimbal.scad` | The earlier gimbal-only model, kept for the encoder detail |
| `renders/` | Rendered views, regenerated by the commands below |
| `*.stl` | Meshes for viewing or import; **see the printing warning** |

## Regenerating

`--colorscheme` is pinned on every line. Left off, OpenSCAD uses
whatever the local default is, and the four stills come back with
different backgrounds — which is exactly what happened once.

```sh
S="--colorscheme=Tomorrow --autocenter --viewall"
xvfb-run -a openscad -D 'view="all"'     $S -o renders/01_full_scene.png        --imgsize=2200,1400 zerodrift_full_assembly.scad
xvfb-run -a openscad -D 'view="rig"'     $S -o renders/02_rig.png               --imgsize=1600,1300 zerodrift_full_assembly.scad
xvfb-run -a openscad -D 'view="head"'    $S -o renders/03_head_detail.png       --imgsize=1500,1150 zerodrift_full_assembly.scad
xvfb-run -a openscad -D 'view="explode"' $S -o renders/04_exploded_labelled.png --imgsize=1700,2000 zerodrift_full_assembly.scad
```

## Checking it

```sh
tools/cad/check_clearance.sh            # sweeps pan -180..180, tilt -25..25
```

It intersects the rig's two kinematic groups — `rig_fixed()` and
`rig_rotating()` — at each pose and measures the **volume** of the
overlap. Volume, not emptiness: a shaft tip meeting the platform it
drives is a mounting interface, and it appears in the intersection as
a coplanar sheet of zero thickness. Anything with real volume is two
parts trying to occupy the same space.

The test drives the *shipped* modules rather than a simplified copy,
so it cannot drift away from the model it guards. That is also why
`geometry.scad` exists: the animation and the still model had already
drifted apart once, and both rendered perfectly while they did.

## Do not print the structural standoff

The STLs are for **viewing and fit-checking**, not for printing every
part. The head standoff specifically must be an **M3 brass standoff**
from the assortment already on the BOM. Printed in PLA at the 42 mm
length this build uses, carrying ~80 g of head:

| Material | Static droop | Natural frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Steel | 54 µrad (0.5 px) | 404 Hz |
| Aluminium / brass | 158 µrad (1.4 px) | 237 Hz |
| **PLA** | **3,109 µrad (28.5 px)** | **53 Hz** |

53 Hz sits on the 47 Hz platform mode `scenarios/ps26169_benchmark.yaml`
injects. A printed standoff is a resonator tuned to the disturbance the
whole design exists to reject.

## What building the model caught

Drawing the parts at real size next to each other surfaced four things
that reading the BOM did not:

1. **The 830-point breadboard does not fit on the base plate.** It is
   165 mm; the 6″ acrylic is 152.4 mm. It belongs on the bench beside
   the rig — which is better practice anyway, since §2E deliberately
   vibrates that plate and the control electronics are the last thing
   that should ride it.
2. **A 32 mm webcam PCB does not fit a 30 mm-tall box.** The head is
   72 × 46 × 42.
3. **The camera has to point out of the enclosure.** An early placement
   had the lens aimed into the box's own interior.
4. **A face-mounted stepper cannot sit shaft-up on a solid plate.** The
   shaft exits the same face that bolts down, so the pan motor hangs
   underneath and the shaft passes through a clearance hole.

## What the clearance sweep caught

Renders did not show any of these. Intersecting the two kinematic
groups showed all of them at once:

5. **Both AS5600s were 12 mm off the axis they were measuring.** The
   arms reached part-way in and the chips were placed at the arms'
   inner ends rather than on the centreline. The AS5600 reads the
   *direction* of the field through its die; off-axis it returns a
   number that is not an angle, and it does so without complaint. Both
   feedback channels would have been dead on a rig that looked
   perfect. The placements are now derived from `geometry.scad`, with
   an `assert` on the air gap.
6. **The pan encoder post was inside the pan rotation.** It turned with
   the magnet it was supposed to measure — an encoder reading a
   constant.
7. **The tilt encoder arm reached down, through the pan platform.**
   It now reaches up over the shaft.
8. **The tilt bracket clipped the platform** because the platform was
   34 mm and the bracket 30 mm wide at 46 mm out. The platform is now
   60 mm, cut from the second sheet in the pack of two — no new part.
9. **The camera's service loop ran through the base plate.** Slack
   cable drawn to z = −4 mm, in 3 mm acrylic.
10. **The head hit the base plate at +17° of tilt.** The head hangs
    65 mm out from the tilt axis, so tilting down swings it into the
    table. The sweep showed it as interference at +25° at *every* pan
    angle at once, which is the signature of a range limit rather than
    a clash with one part. A plain L-bracket puts the tilt axis
    48 mm up; a **14 mm standoff riser** under the bracket lifts it to
    62 mm, which buys the full ±25° — and, as a side effect, lifts the
    whole tilt stage clear of the pan encoder post, so pan is no
    longer limited either.

### Travel

Both limits below are measured by the sweep, not estimated. PS26169's
widest pattern asks for ±35° of pan and a few degrees of elevation, so
neither costs us anything — but they are real limits of the layout and
belong in the firmware as soft stops.

| Axis | Soft-limit to | Measured | Set by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan | ±120° | clear at −120°; first contact at −150° | the tilt stage reaching the fixed encoder post |
| Tilt | ±25° | clear at ±25° | the head swinging toward the base plate |

±120° is the conservative figure: it is the largest angle the sweep has
actually shown clear, not an interpolation toward the −150° contact.
Re-run `tools/cad/check_clearance.sh 10` if you want the boundary
resolved more finely than the 30° grid.

Before the 14 mm riser, pan was bounded at +30° and tilt at +17°, which
is what made the riser worth four M3 standoffs from the assortment.

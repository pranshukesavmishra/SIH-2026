ABOUT US

? WHAT AM I LOOKING AT?

Real-time optical tracker verification guide

Two laser terminals must point at each other with micro-radian accuracy before they can talk. Our software finds the other terminal's blinking beacon in a noisy sky and steers a virtual camera to keep it centred. This page replays a logged run of the real engine, frame by frame — nothing here is animated by hand.

⌖ VIEWPORT — the camera's view. Glowing dot = the beacon at its recorded position. Arcs around it = our tracker's lock reticle, coloured by state. Red ◇ = decoys (only the "Decoy field" scenario has any). The beacon barely moves — that stillness is the achievement: the mount is following it.

▮ CONSOLE — the big number is the pointing error in µrad (1 µrad ≈ 1 mm sideways per km). Below: how many bright spots were seen, the beacon's signal strength, the 4 Hz blink test score, the AI verifier's confidence, how hard the mount is turning, and compute time per 33 ms frame.

STATES — SEARCH spiral-scanning · ACQUIRE candidate found, confirming · TRACK locked · COAST beacon blinked off, pointing from prediction · REACQUIRE lost, recovering. COAST is normal — the beacon is dark half of every blink cycle.

CHARTS — the story over time. Error dives after acquisition and stays ~200 µrad, far below the red FOV-loss line. Click any chart to jump the replay there.

HOW TO CHECK IT'S REAL — tiles must read 1.27 s · 98.3 % · 198 µrad · 100 % on the ISS pass; the constants line must say FOV 6°, 30 fps, blink 4 Hz, slew limit 20°/s; every number regenerates from one command in the open-source repo.

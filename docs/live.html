LIVE · BEACON IDENTIFICATION

Identify a beacon by its blink, not its brightness

This runs the identification stage of our coarse-alignment pipeline on your own webcam, in the browser. Point a blinking light at the camera and it locks on. Point a steady light — a lamp, a screen, a torch left on — and it stays silent, however bright it is. That refusal is the whole point: in free-space optical links the terminal must find its beacon among every other bright thing in the field, and brightness alone cannot tell them apart. Modulation can.

Your camera feed is processed entirely on this device. Nothing is recorded, uploaded, or sent anywhere.

HOW TO RUN IT

  1. Press Start Camera and allow access.
  2. Open your phone's flashlight and blink it at the camera, roughly 4 times a second, from about a metre away.
  3. Watch it go SEARCHING → CONFIRMING → LOCKED. The lock ring turns green.
  4. Now hold the torch steady. The lock drops — a constant light is not a beacon.
  5. Set the target rate to a different value and blink at the old one. It refuses: it is matched to a specific signature, not to "something bright".
What this shares with the flight software. The same principle drives the simulator, the desk rig and this page: sample brightness at every candidate on every frame — including the frames where the beacon is dark, because the off-half of the cycle carries half the signature — then require both a real modulation depth and a matching frequency before declaring a lock. What differs is only the front end that finds candidates in the first place, which is matched to the optics it actually looks through.

Not needed in this design

No bearings, belts or pulleys. No angle sensors, no splitter. No breadboard, no soldering. No metal shaft hubs (the hubs are printed and clamp with a set screw).

Why it is cheaper than the belt design

The belt design needs 4 bearings, 3 pulleys, 4 belts and 1,074 g of printing. The tower needs none of those parts and prints 198 g.

The order that saves time

  1. Today: send the coupon STL to the print shop. It is small and quick.
  2. Same day: buy the electronics and the screw kit.
  3. When the coupon fits: print the six parts. The parts take about 14–20 hours.
  4. Wire the UNO and CNC shield while the printer runs, and test each motor on the bench.
Safety. The laser is 5 mW, red. Never look into the beam and never point it at a person or at a shiny surface. The 12 V adapter needs an undamaged cable and a fuse. Never plug or unplug a motor or a driver with 12 V on.

6 · Build steps 1–5

Mechanics

1
Print and check the coupon. Print the test coupon. A NEMA17 shaft must slide into the 5.2 mm hole, an M3 screw must bite in the small hole, an M3 nut must drop into the slot, the motor pilot must sit in the 22 mm hole, the camera board must lie in the pocket and the laser must push into the round hole.
CHECK: All six fits are right. If one is not, tell us which, and we change that one number.
2
Print the six parts. Use the print job sheet. 198 g in total.
CHECK: Every part is flat where it should be and the holes are clean.
3
Pan motor onto the cap. Put the motor flange against the underside of the cap. The shaft goes up through the round hole. Fit 4 × M3 × 8 from the top.
CHECK: The shaft sticks out about 20 mm above the cap.
4
Cap and motor onto the sleeve. Feed the motor cable out through the slot. Lower the motor into the sleeve. Fit 4 × M3 × 12 into the four corners (they cut their own thread; do not over-tighten).
CHECK: The cap lies flat, with no gap.
5
Sleeve onto the base. Push 4 nuts into the pockets under the base. Stand the sleeve on the pad. Fit 4 × M3 × 12 from the top.
CHECK: The tower stands straight. The cable slot faces away from the head side.

Inside view: the pan motor hangs from the cap, the tower sleeve is hidden.

6 · Build steps 6–10

Top half and finishing

6
Pan hub. Drop a nut into the hub slot. Slide the hub onto the shaft, wide end up. Turn it so the set screw meets the flat side of the shaft. Tighten 1 × M3 × 8. Leave 1 mm above the cap.
CHECK: Turn the hub by hand: the shaft turns with it. It does not rub the cap.
7
Tilt mount on the hub. Put 3 × M3 × 10 into the plate (the heads sit in the round pockets on top). Screw them down into the hub. The shaft tip goes into the small pocket under the plate.
CHECK: The plate is level and turns with the shaft.
8
Tilt motor. Slide the motor in from the back, flat on the plate, its round boss into the hole in the wall. Fit 4 × M3 × 8 from the front of the wall. The cable points backwards.
CHECK: The shaft sticks out about 20 mm beyond the wall.
9
Head, camera and laser. Drop a nut into the head slot. Slide the head on the tilt shaft until it is 1–2 mm from the wall. Tighten 1 × M3 × 8 on the flat. Mark and pre-drill the camera board corners, then fix it with 4 × M2 × 6 (or a little glue). Push in the laser and tighten the top clamp screw.
CHECK: With the power off, the head swings by hand about ±45° without touching anything.
10
Wire it, flash it, test it. Follow the next two pages: plug-in wiring, then the firmware and the tests.
CHECK: The Live page says the rig is connected.

Head, camera and laser, close up.

7 · Wiring and firmware

Plug it in

Put the firmware on the UNO

  1. Plug the UNO into the laptop with the USB cable. Power off the 12 V.
  2. Run tools/rig/flash.sh tower. It builds the direct-drive version (ratio 1:1, pan ±90°, tilt ±30°, gentle speed).
  3. The board prints "# ZeroDrift Mk2 ready, encoders=no, belts=1:1". That line means it worked.

Set the Live page

  1. Rig type: MK2 · steppers. Drive: Direct drive (1:1).
  2. Press CONNECT RIG, then START CAMERA. Turn on the 12 V.
  3. Press TEST MOTION: pan and tilt must move the way the picture on screen moves. If one goes the wrong way, flip the motor plug, or use INVERT.
  4. Switch on the laser and press SET AIM on the laser dot.
Driver current. Fit all 3 jumpers under each A4988 (1/16 step). Turn the small screw on each driver until the motor is strong enough not to skip steps. Keep the driver only warm. If a motor is too hot to touch, turn it down.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
A motor buzzes and does not turnCheck the 4-pin plug sits fully in. Check the 12 V is on. Turn the driver current up a little.
A motor turns the wrong wayFlip that motor's plug, or use INVERT on the Live page.
A hub or the head slips on its shaftThe set screw must press on the flat side. Tighten it. Add a drop of thread-lock.
The head drops or shakesThe camera and laser are too heavy or off-centre. Keep the head under about 150 g.
It loses position (centre is not centre)Turn the driver current up a little or lower the speed. Check the jumpers (1/16).
A driver gets too hotFit the heatsink, turn the current down, add a small fan.

If you need finer aim later

One step is 0.11°, about 2 pixels on the MK2 camera. For 0.056°, use DRV8825 drivers at 1/32 (set MICROSTEPS to 32 in the firmware). For 0.028° use the belt design, Plan Urena. The UNO, shield, motors and firmware carry over.
